### Highest-impact fixes

1. **Presentation.** Keep **Next** in a fixed position on the 375×667 win card. Combine time and medal into one badge, and give adoption or a ready build the main reward row. Put replay details behind the thumbnail. Check the layout in English and Turkish.
2. **Appeal.** Redraw the orange tabby at board scale with shoulders, a rounder belly, and a tapered rump. Wrap its stripes around that shape. Test a long straight cat and a tight bend before applying the treatment to other breeds.
3. **Juice.** Profile six rapid sends and an x3 chain on a low-end Android. If compositing dominates, try lowering the DPR of the moving canvas alone. Use **33 ms p95 or better during those sequences** as the first target and verify that tap feedback stays immediate.
4. **Fun.** Curate three boards in levels 5–15 around distinct decisions. Build one deliberate last-cushion chain, one board where the obvious early park loses, and one recovery board that lets players try a different move on the same layout. Watch first-time players and see whether they can explain the bench rule and use it on the next board. Preserve existing dare links when changing campaign layouts.
5. **Retention.** Carry one named resident’s next payoff from the map through the win card to the room. Show the exact remaining action, then put that resident’s first request on the arrival card. Measure whether players finish that thread during the first two sessions.
6. **Virality.** Use the same production HTTPS URL for the typed code and QR. Keep **Dare a friend** as the immediate link action and label video preparation explicitly. On iMessage and WhatsApp, test link-only and caption-stripped sends through to the friend’s first move and counter-dare, including opening the clip on the receiving phone.
7. **Overall.** Prioritize the early-board pass and the low-end phone test together. The next scoring milestone is a new player setting up a chain on purpose, seeing it resolve smoothly, and reaching the next board in one tap.
