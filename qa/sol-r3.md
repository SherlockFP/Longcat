## 5/10 combined for fun, long-term retention, and virality

I’d play through Mochi moving in. I see little reason to return in week three and only one moment I might send to a friend. The best idea is the bench: a cat that looks like a mistake now can complete the next carrier and set off another departure. That gives the player a chance to feel clever and gives the game a payoff worth watching. The frames bury it under small previews, a generic “Purrfect!” banner, and a café whose first big reward is five extra coins per win. The cats are expressive and the art already has a recognizable look. The biggest weakness is how rarely the game asks the player to make an interesting choice and then shows why it mattered. This is a design judgment from the spec and stills. Animation feel and actual retention are unverified.

## Eight fixes, in priority order

1. **Make the bench a decision by Level 3.** Level 1 shows three orange cats with clear upward exits. Level 2 shows a blue cat waiting for a one-cat orange carrier to leave. Both teach the rules, but neither makes me think. Hand-build Level 3 so the player can send either a tempting off-color cat or a useful off-color cat that frees a needed lane. Both moves should be recoverable. The useful move should set up an automatic boarding chain the player can see coming. Keep the kitten teach to one clean beat, then build Levels 4–10 around choices like this.

2. **Make the next two carriers readable in one glance.** In the Level 12 departure frame, the outgoing green carrier obscures the incoming pink one and partly covers the count. Those are the exact things I need to plan my next tap. Give **Now** and the next two carriers fixed positions, with a pattern badge and fill pips on each. Put departing carriers on a separate animation path. While I’m choosing a cat, show whether it will board or which cushion it will occupy. The bench should feel like a resource I chose to spend.

3. **Reject generated boards that are solvable and dull.** The generator proves one route works and calls for some detours. Add acceptance checks for what the player actually faces: how often two plausible exits have meaningfully different bench costs, whether the safer move has a visible clue, and how many taps are forced cleanup. Audit alternate routes under the real boarding rules. The dog teaching frame also says “Dog wakes when 3 cats leave,” while the spec says **nearby**. Highlight the cells that count and tick down only when an eligible cat leaves. A trap is fair when I can explain it after one attempt.

4. **Build the share moment around cats boarding in sequence.** In the departure frame, the huge “Purrfect!” covers the top row while the carrier stack hides the action. For a two-carrier chain, briefly hold the next input, show the waiting cats hop off their cushions one by one, snap the door, and let the second departure land on a higher note. Keep the board and bench visible throughout. Add an opt-in, silent-readable replay share on exceptional chains, with the setup tap included so a viewer can follow the payoff.

5. **Bring the good toys forward.** If a player has to wait until Level 25 for yarn and Level 40 for a mystery reveal, too much of the opening is variations on the same exit check. Put a dog teach around Level 8 and a yarn teach around Level 12. Follow each with a level where the mechanic changes which cat you should send first. Save the harder combinations for later. A dog that predictably opens a lane matters more than a dog that simply wakes up.

6. **Give Mochi a perk the player can feel on the next board.** The arrival card spends its biggest moment on `+5 coins on every win`, then asks me to press Play. Put a free Hint from Mochi in the player’s hands on Level 6 and show its source on the booster. In the café, let players tap a resident to see their favorite spot, a short idle interaction, and their perk. Ship a playable third area before calling the café a months-long progression system. The first two areas offer twelve purchases and four arrivals, then the build hits “Coming soon.”

7. **Make the loss screen explain the loss before it sells the rescue.** Level 49 shows a full bench, a prominent ad button, and a 60-coin option the player can’t afford with 50. Show the overflow cat, all occupied seats, and the current carrier’s remaining demand at the top of the offer. Describe exactly what the recovery does in player terms, such as “Undo the green cat, then scoop any cat.” Keep Retry visible at the same hierarchy as the recovery actions. Players should leave the screen knowing what move they’d change.

8. **Make Cat Show worth returning for without making it another quota.** The weekly plan currently asks players to win ordinary levels for ribbons, on top of daily tasks and a login calendar. Give each theme one authored challenge that changes how the puzzle plays. For example, Pajama Party could use a sleeping-dog board with a bonus for clearing it with two cushions still free. Offer unlimited practice and make the exclusive décor attainable through the challenge. That gives players a specific puzzle to come back for and a result they can compare.
