### The eight most important remaining fixes

1. **Make the long cat the payoff.** Rework one Level 1 send until the cat visibly crouches, stretches across the gap, lands paws-first, and folds into the carrier with a tail snap. Bring the soft texture of the faces into the bodies. Let that landing drive the counter pop, sound, and haptic. Use that exact beat as the lead store visual.

2. **Make the first four boards prove the game’s range.** Keep the authored sequence. Aim for the first carrier departure within ten seconds, make both Level 2 bench cats visibly wake up when their carrier arrives, and pulse the exit that the Level 3 kitten frees. At Level 4, watch whether new players choose the earlier carrier unaided. Track Level 4 starts and Level 10 reach.

3. **Make the next move readable on a crowded board.** On press, connect the cat’s exit, first blocker, and projected destination. Use the same pattern symbol on the cat and its carrier. Flag the final free cushion. Keep chain text clear of the carrier mouth, and give tutorial copy and status messages one shared lane. Level 12 already shows what happens when both speak at once.

4. **Make wins fast and losses teach.** Give ordinary clears a compact result with one thumb-level action, **Next** or **Build** when the upgrade is ready. Let the earned star fly into the build meter. On failure, keep the bench and queue visible, point to the overflowing cat, and say how far away its carrier is. Keep **Take back** and **Retry** clear of the life-loss toast.

5. **Give the hub one obvious reason to act.** Keep Play pinned and spotlight one meaningful action in the room. Put the tip jar on the counter and the ready request over its resident. A prompt like **Help Mochi earn his beanie** gives the player a reason to care. The right rail, goal strip, and Shop badge currently compete with that reason.

6. **Extend the house players came to build.** Show the next floor early, then ship a chapter beyond the rooftop and a visible renovation tier. Give each season’s visitor a furnished spot, an arrival scene, and an idle behavior. The first invitation at eight stamps is a good milestone. Let players see what inviting Pip will change.

7. **Give each challenge a clear job.** Make Daily a curated, free-retry board with a forgiving streak. Lead weekly Events with Cat Show’s seat limit and chain goals, and show gold progress during the run. Make Rush about beating your own time, with a visible clock, a personal-best split, and **Run again** on the result. The current Rush result leads with **Cat Show**.

8. **Turn the replay into a playable dare.** Frame the nearly full bench, the setup tap, and the cascade in a short vertical clip that reads with sound off. Pair it with **Beat my chain ×4** or **Beat my time** and a link to the exact board. Test whether a recipient can explain the trick after one view and start the challenge from the link.
