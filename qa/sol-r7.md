**6.5/10 combined.** Long Cat Out! is most fun when a move that looks reckless clears the bench and sends two carriers away. That’s a payoff worth building the game around. The eight residents and distinct café rooms give early wins somewhere to go, but by Level 61 the rooftop is mostly a repeat purchase for coins and a Scoop. The chain replay captures the right event, yet the win screen gives it the smallest stage. I reran the 600-level core check and the café economy check. Both passed. This is a design score based on the frames and code. Player retention and sharing remain unmeasured.

1. **Make the next move readable at a glance.** In the Level 5 frame, the orange carrier’s `1/3` floats over the belt, green hangs off the right edge as a tiny badge, and bench occupancy has to be counted by eye. Put three fill pips on the active carrier, label the bench `4/5`, and put every upcoming pattern in one numbered queue. The press preview already simulates the move, but it draws every carrier destination over the current carrier. Fix the case where Mom fills it and the kitten boards the next one. At phone size, a player should be able to point to both destinations before releasing. [Move preview and queue](/C:/Users/Admin/Documents/Msjelly/studio/long-cat-out/src/game/game.js:408)

2. **Generate distinct puzzles, not more variations of the same squeeze.** The delayed traps are real: 445 of 455 eligible normal levels have one. But normal levels average 18.9% losing taps at L100–249 and 19.0% at L250–600, measured along the generator’s winning line, and 94 generated levels miss their full difficulty band. Build chapter templates around different decisions: open a corridor, time a two-seat pair, or stage a carrier entirely from the bench. Sample several plausible winning routes, measure how long a bad choice takes to become legible, and make the difficulty-band limits actual acceptance criteria for the curated campaign. `bandScore` currently treats the upper losing-tap limit as a penalty. 3. **Make Levels 3–7 teach planning through consequences.** Keep L3’s recoverable pink-pair choice. When they land, mark both cushions with the pink carrier’s queue position so the player sees how long those seats are tied up. Curate L5–7 so one board rewards parking for the next carrier, one rewards holding a pair back, and one lets the player deliberately set up a full carrier from the bench. Put the first free recovery right after the player sees which earlier choice caused the jam. [Opening boards](/C:/Users/Admin/Documents/Msjelly/studio/long-cat-out/src/core/generate.js:121)

4. **Cut the fail sheet in half.** The L49 screen shows the stranded cat, then dims the clues and gives an ad the brightest tile. Keep the bench, queue, and offending cat at full brightness. Trace the cat to the missing seat and highlight its carrier’s actual queue position. Put **Take Back** and **Retry** side by side in a shallow drawer, with Scoop, the ad, and the coin offer behind **More options**. The screen should explain the mistake in one glance. Give that loop the full width of the win card on a strong chain, with Share directly below. Replace the 240-pixel export with a phone-readable portrait clip and verify that it actually sends from iOS and Android.
