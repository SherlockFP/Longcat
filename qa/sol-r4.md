**6/10 combined for fun, long-term retention, and virality.** The carrier queue gives a simple tap-away puzzle a real planning hook, and the café finally looks like somewhere I’d want to bring a cat home to. But the opening frame asks me to send three orange cats to an orange carrier, the most shareable frame puts a giant “Meow-velous!” over the bench where the payoff happens, and the weekly event in the spec rewards playing more of the same levels. I’d play another board. The current design gives me little reason to come back next month or send a friend a clip. That’s a design judgment from the supplied frames, spec, and round-three review. Animation feel and player behavior remain unverified.

1. **Make Level 3 the first board worth talking about.** Keep Level 1 as a quick control lesson. Hand-build Level 3 with two legal off-color taps. One parks a pink cat that will help fill the next carrier. The other burns the same seat but opens a different route. Make both recoverable and let the better setup visibly trigger a two-carrier chain. Hand-author Levels 4–10 around different decisions, including a kitten pair whose two seats matter. The generator’s forced bench detours make players use the bench. This sequence should make them think about *which* cat gets the seat.

2. **Make the next tap predictable at a glance.** In the Level 12 departure frame, four carriers are visible, the incoming pink carrier competes with the departing green one, and the headline covers the cushions. Give **Now**, **Next**, and **Then** fixed positions with large matching pattern badges. Attach three fill pips to **Now** and animate departures clear of those slots. Use press-and-hold to preview the exit lane and destination, including both destinations for a linked pair. On Level 55, that same preview should highlight the neighbors that count toward the ice’s **2**. A player should be able to explain the result before committing the tap.

3. **Reject solvable boards that have a boring move order.** The revised color quotas help, but four colors can still produce a board that plays itself. Extend the solver to compare legal branches. For a normal board after the tutorial, require at least two decisions where legal moves produce different bench pressure, and flag boards that spend more than half their taps on forced cleanup. For hard boards, require a tempting legal move that increases the risk of overflow and a clue the player can see in the queue or on the board. In the yarn teaching level, make knocking the ball away change which cat the player should send next. Give dog, mystery, and ice the same standard.

4. **Make the chain the spectacle and the share payload.** Show the setup tap, the first carrier filling, the parked cats hopping into the next one, and the second carrier leaving. Keep the board, bench, and queue visible throughout. Give the second departure a bigger sound and motion payoff, and briefly hold input so another tap can’t bury it. Save a short portrait replay of the best cascade and preview it from the win card. It should make sense muted. The current “Phew!” card has a café progress bar and a Share button, but the actual achievement is offscreen.

5. **Turn Cat Show into a weekly puzzle people can compare.** Replace the ribbon grind with one authored board everyone gets, unlimited practice, and a result based on peak bench occupancy. Award the weekly décor for clearing a published target, such as winning without exceeding four occupied seats. Give the result a challenge link so a friend can try the same board. This gives the event a reason to exist alongside daily tasks and gives players something specific to send each other.

6. **Make the café pay off on the first visit and ship enough of it to sustain repeat play.** Keep the revised 1–3 star prices and early Mochi unlock. At the first café visit, put an affordable build at the top, animate it into the room immediately, and show Mochi using it when he moves in. The Sunny Window frame has warm lighting and a cat with personality, but at one star every visible purchase is disabled. Surface the cheapest reachable task across rooms on the win card. Ship a playable third area and fold the legacy Cozy Room breeds into café visits so collection and renovation build toward the same place.

7. **Make a full bench feel like a loss the player can learn from.** In Level 49, the game asks for an ad or coins while the board is dimmed and the failed green cat is stranded on the belt. First freeze the moment with the overflow cat, all three occupied cushions, and the current carrier’s purple star clearly visible. Show what Scoop will remove and which seat it frees. Give **Retry** the same visual weight as the rescue options, and show **120 coins** beside the player’s **50** before routing them to the shop. A player who retries should already know which decision they want to change.

8. **Spend the next art pass on cat motion.** The faces now have distinct expressions, and the café shows the cast can carry a scene. On the board, the long bodies still read as rigid pieces. Give a sent cat a head-first launch, visible running paws, a bend that travels down its body, and a tail that catches up on landing. Give a bonk a quick recoil that points straight at the blocker. Use Level 1’s three nearly identical sends as the test. By the third, tapping a cat should still feel good.
