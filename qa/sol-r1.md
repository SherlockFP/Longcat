**5/10 combined for fun, long-term retention, and virality.** The best idea is that a cat you parked three moves ago can suddenly help fill a carrier. That gives a simple tap puzzle room for foresight and a satisfying payoff. But the early frames teach the procedure without showing off that payoff, the Level 30 frame makes it hard to read the consequences of a tap, and the café’s first resident pays out a tiny currency bonus. The Level 12 departure frame shows the most shareable moment in the game, yet the celebration text competes with the cats that make it worth watching. The art already has personality. I’m scoring the spec and the supplied frames, so timing and actual player behavior are unverified.

1. **Make Level 2 end with a chain reaction the player set up.** Give the blue carrier a two-cat capacity for this tutorial. Put two free blue cats on the board before the last orange goes in. Highlight the first blue tap, then let the player choose whether to park the second before finishing orange. If they do, both blues jump in and the second carrier departs without another tap. The current “Wrong color?” message teaches players to see the bench as a penalty. This level should teach them how to use it.

Give the chevrons a consistent pocket of clear space and stamp each carrier with its cats’ fur pattern, including on the previews. On press, draw the exit lane and highlight the front carrier or the exact cushion the cat would use. On a blocked tap, stop the lane at the blocker. Keep it a single tap and make the cue part of the response.

Make Level 25’s teaching board demonstrate the yarn payoff in one move by rolling it away and opening a second cat’s exit.
