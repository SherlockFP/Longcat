## 5/10 combined

**I’d play through the tutorial and meet Mochi. I don’t see enough in v2 to make me come back for months or send a clip to a friend.** The five-seat bench is the best idea in the game. Picking which cat to free now and which to park for later can turn a simple tap-away board into a real puzzle. But the first two levels mostly teach where cats go, and the Level 12 payoff frame gives more space to “Purrfect!” than to the cats doing something worth watching. The café has a strong resident reveal, then asks me to buy furniture for an empty room. The weekly show, streaks and dailies mostly pay me to play more of the same. This is a design score from the spec and stills. Tap feel, animation timing and actual retention are unverified.

## The eight fixes, in order

1. **Put the first clever chain in Level 3.** Start with one pink cat on the bench and show pink with two slots after orange. Give the player a choice between freeing an irrelevant blue cat and parking the second pink. When they park pink and finish orange, both pinks jump in and the second carrier leaves. Let the player discover the move. The first two levels already teach tapping and parking. Level 3 should make the player feel smart for using both.

2. **Build levels around decisions, and reject filler boards.** The generator guarantees a winning order with bench room to spare. That tells you almost nothing about whether the player had an interesting choice. Analyze reachable states in the solver. Flag boards where safe taps are interchangeable for most of the solve. For hard boards, require at least one fork where two cats can leave, one frees a needed lane, and the other spends bench space the player will need soon. Put the clue in the visible carrier queue and board geometry. Hand-author the first ten levels to set the quality bar.

3. **Make the cats the payoff.** In the Level 12 frame, the empty board and giant text get the spotlight while the departing carrier crowds the queue. On a chain, let players see the bench cats perk up, sprint and leap in sequence, then snap the door shut and roll the carrier out in its own space. Save the biggest celebration for two or more departures. Capture a six-second clip from the decisive tap through the last departure and watch it muted. The clip should make sense and get a laugh without the game’s UI explaining it.

4. **Make Level 49 readable at a glance.** The ~47 px cells clear the spec’s size floor, yet faces, paws, chevrons, mystery badges and yarn all compete for the same space. The active carrier has a count but no clear visual priority over the previews, and a fourth carrier peeks in at the edge. Give the active carrier three large fill dots and the matching fur symbol, show exactly two subdued previews, and repeat the symbol on occupied cushions. Give direction markers a clear patch of space in front of each face. On a bonk, trace the lane and pulse the first blocker. Test at 375 px in grayscale and ask players to point to what can leave and what will board.

5. **Put the free recovery first on the bench-full screen.** The modal’s loudest button is a rewarded ad, the 90-coin option is disabled with 50 coins in the bank, and the player’s two Scoops sit lower down. Lead with **“Undo and scoop · 2 left”** when one is available and explain which move filled the bench. When the fifth cushion fills, make it pulse and briefly label the next tap that would overflow it. Save the paid seat for a player who can see exactly what that extra seat buys.

6. **Give Mochi something to do in the café.** The arrival card is the strongest character moment in the set. The next thing I should see is Mochi testing the lamps, curling up on the counter, or greeting me when I come back. In the hub, replace generic covered markers with silhouettes of the actual upcoming décor and show which purchase brings the next resident. Carry the perk into the moment it pays out with a small **“Mochi +5”** on the win screen. Make the cat I just met feel like part of the game I’m going back to.

7. **Move the toys forward and teach them through play.** Kittens give you a double boarding early, then the spec waits until Level 15 for the dog and Level 25 for yarn. Trial a hand-built dog level around L8 and a yarn level around L12. Give each mechanic one clear setup, then use it in a real carrier-order decision within the next two levels. Keep the relaxed boards after difficulty spikes. By L15 the player should have seen several different ways a board can surprise them.

8. **Turn Cat Show into a weekly puzzle worth sharing.** The current ten-step ribbon track rewards volume. Give everyone the same weekly seeded board with a fixed booster loadout and a visible goal such as *finish with two empty cushions*. Rotate the mechanic with the theme. Award the décor for solving the challenge and use the resulting chain clip as the share card, with the board code so a friend can try the same puzzle. That gives players a reason to come back next week and a specific thing to send someone.
